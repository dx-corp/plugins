# Evidence limits for legal drafts

Use the customer's approved instructions and the sources actually read in this
task. An upstream practice profile, placeholder or sample negotiating position
does not establish the customer's policy.

- Cite document facts to their actual version and clause, page or section.
- Cite a proposed customer position to an approved playbook or explicit
  instruction. Do not invent fallback periods, exceptions or thresholds.
- A claim about law requires the relevant jurisdiction and a current authorized
  source that was actually checked. A citation to a contract clause does not
  support a claim about enforceability or legal requirements.
- Claims about standard, customary or typical market terms require supplied or
  verified comparative evidence. Do not invent a market baseline or disguise
  an unsupported recommendation as an illustrative default.

When jurisdiction, client side or approved positions are missing, return only
clause-supported observations, conditional operational questions and decisions
for counsel. Do not describe a term as unenforceable, legally disfavored or
contrary to a market standard without the required evidence. State what remains
unverified and which inputs counsel must supply. Keep the output a draft for
attorney review, or a supervised educational exercise where appropriate.

If a prior draft overstates its support, explicitly withdraw the unsupported
claims and correct the saved draft. A warning at the end does not cure an
unsupported conclusion in the body.
